The Best Movies According to Angela

My top3 movies of all-time

Pegasus 3

This isn't just a racing film—it's a midlife confession of a generation.

Zhang Chi is still driving, but now he drives for something bigger than himself. The former rebel has become the backbone of his team and a mentor to young drivers.When AI threatens to replace human racers and the era of internal combustion is fading, he chooses the riskiest strategy to defend what algorithms can never replace: passion. Han Han spent seven years on this trilogy, finally capturing what it means to be born in the 80s—no longer reckless youth, but the fire inside hasn't gone out.

Cry Me a Sad River

A film that will leave you breathless from crying—not for sentimentality, but because the pain is unbearably real.

Yi Yao"s story is like a thorn, stuck in the darkest corners of youth. Rumors cut deeper than knives, and watching in silence is crueler than bullying itself.When she turns back at the seaside and says, "Every one of you is a murderer," I finally understood: in school bullying, there are no innocent bystanders. Ren Min's performance makes you forget this is fiction. The damp alleyways, the gray classrooms, the red words that won't wash away—youth should be bright, but for some, it's only a river of sorrow running backward.

Full River Red

Zhang Yimou isn't telling the story of great heroes this time—he's telling the story of nobodies.

It's four years after Yue Fei's death, and Qin Hui still holds power. A few insignificant people—a soldier, a dancer, a watchman—risk everything for a poem. Was it worth it? You'll leave the theater in silence.Shen Teng surprised me. His character Zhang Da is timid as a quail most of the time. But when he strips off his shirt to reveal "Serve the Country with Utmost Loyalty" tattooed on his back, the entire theater falls silent. Some things matter more than life.The ending is genius. The entire army recites "Full River Red" in unison—no clashing swords, no bloodshed, just thousands of voices echoing through the valley. In that moment, it hits you: killing Qin Hui wasn't the point. Making sure "Full River Red" survives—that's the point. People die, but spirit endures.
